## 1. Welcome to the Cocoon

Welcome back, and welcome to the Cocoon.

Here's why we start here, with the inside, before we ever talk about your body, your money or your career.

A caterpillar doesn't become a butterfly by working harder at being a caterpillar. Something has to happen on the inside first, quietly, out of sight. The cocoon isn't a pause in the story. It's where the story changes.

So for the next three weeks we go inward: your **Spiritual Life**, your **Character**, and your **Emotional Life**. Everything else in your Charter will grow from here.

## 2. What we mean by "spiritual life"

Let me say this clearly, because it matters: **this week is for everyone.**

Maybe you have a faith tradition that's carried you your whole life. Maybe you're rebuilding faith after a season when it didn't hold. Maybe the word "spiritual" makes you a little uneasy, and what moves you is nature, or music, or your grandmother's kitchen, or the quiet before sunrise. Maybe you'd call yourself none of the above.

You belong here, all of you.

In LifeCharter, your spiritual life is **your relationship with whatever is larger than you, and what that relationship gives your life: meaning, direction, and a place to rest.**

You might call it God. Spirit. Source. The Universe. Love. Your ancestors. Something without a name. You don't need my words for it. You have your own, and your own are the ones that count.

I won't teach you your faith. You're the one who knows your tradition and your own heart. What I'll do is ask good questions and make room for you to hear your own answers.

## 3. Movement 1: The Air I'm In

Let's start with honest sight.

Think about your spiritual life as it is right now. Not as it was at its best. Not as you think it should be. As it is.

Maybe it feels full and alive. Maybe it's dry, and you're going through the motions. Maybe it's tangled up with old hurt: something you were taught, someone who used faith as a weapon, a loss that made you question everything. Maybe it's just been crowded out by a busy life.

None of those are failures. They're the air you're in, and you can only renegotiate with air you're willing to see.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: The Air I'm In**
> 1. On a scale of 1 to 10, my spiritual life today is a ___. (Look back at your Week 0 snapshot. Has anything shifted already?)
> 2. Three words that describe my spiritual life right now are…
> 3. When do I feel most connected to something larger than me? When do I feel furthest away?

## 4. Movement 2: My Truth

Now we get to one of the most freeing parts of this whole program: **My Truth**.

Each of us carries beliefs about the spiritual part of life. Some of them hold us up like wind under a wing. Some of them quietly weigh us down. And many of them we never actually chose. They were handed to us by family, by a community, by a painful experience, or by the culture we grew up in.

Here are some beliefs people carry. See whether any of them feel familiar:

- "I have to earn my place."
- "Doubt means I'm doing it wrong."
- "My spiritual life is private, so I don't need anyone."
- "I'm too busy for this right now."
- "I'm held, even when I can't feel it."
- "There's a purpose in my being here."

Some of those lift. Some of them weigh. Only you can tell which is which for you.

Your job this week isn't to throw everything out. It's to look at each belief and ask three questions:

1. **Is it true for me?**
2. **Is it mine, or was it handed to me?**
3. **Does it lift me, or weigh me down?**

Then you write the truth you choose to live by: a few sentences that say what you actually believe about your spiritual life, in your own words.

If your tradition has words that carry this for you, a scripture, a teaching or a prayer, bring them in. You know them far better than I do. Tell us in the Collective what they mean to you. I'd love to hear.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Truth**
> 1. List the beliefs you carry about your spiritual life, the lifting ones and the heavy ones.
> 2. Next to each one, mark whether it's **mine** or **handed to me**, and whether it **lifts** or **weighs**.
> 3. Write **My Truth**: three to five sentences, in the present tense, that you choose to live by.

## 5. Movement 3: My Horizon

Now let's look up.

**My Horizon** is your spiritual life when it's thriving. Not perfect, thriving.

Close your eyes for a moment if that feels good. Picture an ordinary day a year from now, when your spiritual life is exactly as you'd want it.

How do you begin that day? Where do you find stillness? What does connection feel like in your body? How do you meet a hard moment, like bad news or a difficult person? Who, if anyone, shares this part of life with you? What do you give back?

Write it in the present tense, as if it's already here: "I begin my mornings…", "When things get hard, I…"

Make it specific enough to picture, and honest enough that it's truly yours, not a picture of someone else's spiritual life.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Horizon**
> Describe your spiritual life when it's thriving: a paragraph or two, present tense, specific enough to picture.

## 6. Movement 4: My Why

Why does that horizon matter to you?

This is the movement people skip, and it's the one that carries you when the good intentions of January run out in February. Your why is the lift under your wings.

Think about what a thriving spiritual life would give you: peace, direction, courage, belonging, a way to carry grief, a steadiness your family can feel. And think about what it costs you, and the people you love, if this part of your life stays dry.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Why**
> 1. A thriving spiritual life matters to me because…
> 2. The people who will feel the difference are…
> 3. If nothing changes here, what it costs me is…

## 7. Movement 5: My Flight Plan

Now: how will you get there?

**My Flight Plan** isn't a list of rules. It's a few practices and choices, sized to the life you actually have, that carry you toward your Horizon.

Here's the most important word I can give you this week: **wing-sized**. Not the flight plan of someone with a different life, a different body or a different schedule. Yours.

A few kinds of things belong in a Flight Plan:

- **Daily anchors**: small practices you can come back to, like a moment of stillness, prayer, gratitude, reading, time outdoors, or music. Whatever connects you, in whatever way your tradition or your heart calls for.
- **Weekly rhythms**: community, worship, service, a longer time of reflection.
- **Boundaries**: what you'll protect, and what you'll say no to, to make room.
- **Support**: who travels this part of life with you.

Two or three anchors you'll actually keep are worth far more than ten you'll abandon by Thursday.

## 8. Movement 6: My Next Right Movement

You don't need the whole flight plan to take off.

Pick **one thing** from your Flight Plan that you'll do this week. Make it small enough that you'll really do it, and specific enough that you'll know when you've done it.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Next Right Movement**
> This week, I will… (what, when, and how I'll know I did it)

## 9. Movement 7: Soul Challenge: "Find the Lift"

Your Soul Challenge for this week is called **Find the Lift**.

Once a day for seven days, stop for two minutes and ask: *"Where is the lift in this moment?"* It might be a kindness, some beauty, a feeling of being held, an answered question, a breath that finally slowed down. Write one line about it each day.

At the end of the week, read all seven lines together. Bring what you notice to the Gathering.

## 10. Closing

Here's what you did this week. You looked honestly at the air you're in. You chose your Truth. You named your Horizon and your Why. You built a Flight Plan sized to your wings. And you picked your next right movement.

That's the first page of your Charter.

Bring your questions and your Find the Lift notes to this week's Gathering. And share in the Collective if you feel moved to. Someone there needs exactly what you're discovering.

Next week, we stay in the Cocoon and turn to **Character**: who you are when no one's watching, and who you're becoming.

Head up. Wings out.
