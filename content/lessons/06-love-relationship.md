## 1. Welcome to the Circle

Welcome to **The Circle**.

We did the inside work in the Cocoon. We cared for body and mind in the Vessel. Now we turn to your people. For the next three weeks: your love relationship, your parenting, and your social life.

We start with the closest circle of all: your **Love Relationship**.

## 2. Who this week is for, and a word about safety

Wherever you are in love right now, this week is for you.

If you're in a relationship, this week is about the partnership you're building together. If you're single and open to love, it's about the love you're ready for, and the partner you're becoming. If you're single and content, or grieving a relationship that ended, it's about how you give and receive love, and what you want your heart to know. You'll find your way through every movement.

One more thing, and it matters. If you're in a relationship where you feel unsafe, afraid or controlled, please reach out for help. In the US, you can call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. If you're in danger right now, call 911 or your local emergency number. You deserve to be safe.

## 3. Flying alongside

When I think about love, I think about flight.

A good partner doesn't carry your wings for you, and you don't carry theirs. You don't have to fly in lockstep, wingtip to wingtip, every moment. A good partnership is two people **flying alongside each other**, reading the same air, sometimes one leading and sometimes the other, and choosing, again and again, to stay in the same sky.

That choosing is what this week is about. Love isn't only something that happens to us. It's something we **build**, on purpose, in ordinary days.

## 4. Movement 1: The Air I'm In

Let's look honestly at your love life today. As always, clear sight, not judgment.

If you're partnered, we'll check in on six parts of partnership:

- **Friendship**: do we actually like each other?
- **Communication**: do we talk, and listen?
- **Affection and intimacy**: touch, closeness, desire
- **Trust**: do I feel safe with this person?
- **Fun and play**: do we still laugh together?
- **Shared direction**: are we flying toward the same horizon?

If you're single, rate how you show up in your closest relationships, or simply skip the grid and write instead.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: The Air I'm In**
> 1. On a scale of 1 to 10, my love life today is a ___.
> 2. Rate the six parts of partnership from 1 to 10 (or skip if you're single).
> 3. Three words that describe my love life right now…
> 4. What's going well that I don't want to lose…
> 5. What's hurting, or missing…

## 5. Movement 2: My Truth

Most of what we believe about love, we learned by watching: our parents, our families, the movies, our own first heartbreaks. Some of those lessons were beautiful. Some were painful. Many were never really examined.

See which of these feel familiar:

- "If it's right, it should just be easy."
- "I have to lose myself to keep someone."
- "It's too late for me to find love, or to rebuild it."
- "Conflict means we're failing."
- "Love is a choice I make every day."
- "I deserve to be loved well, and I can learn to love well."

Same three questions: **Is it true for me? Is it mine, or was it handed to me? Does it lift me, or weigh me down?**

Then write **My Truth**: what you choose to believe about love.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Truth**
> 1. List the beliefs you carry about love and partnership.
> 2. Mark each one: **mine** or **handed to me**, **lifts** or **weighs**.
> 3. Write **My Truth**: three to five sentences, present tense.

## 6. Movement 3: My Horizon

Now picture love thriving.

If you're partnered, describe your relationship at its best. How do you greet each other at the end of the day? How do you handle disagreements? How do you show affection? What do you dream about together? What does your home feel like with both of you in it?

If you're single, describe the love you're ready for. Not a checklist of height and job title: what it *feels like* to be with this person, how you treat each other, what you build together.

Then one more question, for everyone: **What do I bring?** Who are you becoming as a partner? What do you offer, and what are you growing in?

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Horizon**
> 1. Describe love thriving in my life, in the present tense.
> 2. What I bring, and the partner I'm becoming:

## 7. Movement 4: My Why

Why does this matter to you?

A loving partnership is one of the great sources of joy, strength and healing in a life. It shapes your home, your children if you have them, your health, even how long you live. And the love you give and receive changes you.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Why**
> 1. A thriving love life matters to me because…
> 2. The people who will feel the difference are…
> 3. If nothing changes here, what it costs me is…

## 8. Movement 5: My Flight Plan

Love grows through small, repeated acts of care. Your Flight Plan has four parts. If you're single, adapt each one to preparing for love and to your closest relationships.

- **Time together**: rituals that protect your connection. A weekly date, a daily ten-minute check-in, coffee before the day starts, phones away at dinner.
- **How we talk**: how you'll listen, raise hard things, and repair after conflict. "When we argue, we'll take a break if we need one, and always come back."
- **Affection and appreciation**: how you'll show love in the ways your partner actually receives it, and say out loud what you appreciate.
- **Support**: who helps your relationship grow: a counselor, a mentor couple, a class, your faith community, good friends who cheer for you both.

## 9. Movement 6: My Next Right Movement

One thing, this week.

Plan the date. Have the conversation you've been avoiding. Put the phones in a basket at dinner. Book the counseling session. If you're single, write the letter to the love you're ready for, or say yes to the invitation.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Next Right Movement**
> This week, I will… (what, when, and how I'll know I did it)

## 10. Movement 7: Soul Challenge: "Say What You See"

Your Soul Challenge this week is called **Say What You See**.

Each day for seven days, notice one specific thing you appreciate about your partner, and tell them. Not "you're great". Something specific: "I saw how patient you were with your mom on the phone today." "Thank you for making the coffee before I woke up."

If you're single, do this with someone you love, a friend or family member. Then add one thing you appreciate about yourself, because the way you love yourself shapes the love you'll accept.

Write one line each day: what you noticed, and how they responded.

## 11. Closing

Here's what you did this week. You looked honestly at your love life. You chose what you believe about love. You pictured love thriving, and named what you bring. And you built a plan to keep choosing each other, or to get ready for the love that's coming.

That's the sixth chapter of your Charter, and we're halfway through the dimensions.

Bring your Say What You See notes to this week's Gathering. How did they respond, and how did it change you?

Next week, we stay in the Circle with **Parenting**: whether you're raising children, loving grandchildren, mentoring young people, or caring for the child you once were.

Head up. Wings out.
