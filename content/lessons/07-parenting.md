## 1. Welcome back to the Circle

Welcome back to the Circle.

Last week we looked at your love relationship. This week we turn to the young lives you influence, and to the young life you once were. This is **Parenting**.

I know some of you saw that word and thought, *this week isn't for me.* Please stay. I promise it is.

## 2. Three ways through this week

Parenting, in LifeCharter, means **the way you help young lives grow**. There are three ways through this week. Choose the one that fits you, or more than one.

**1. Raising children.** Birth children, adopted, foster, step or bonus children. Little ones, teenagers, or grown children with families of their own. Parenting doesn't end at eighteen. It changes shape.

**2. Influencing young lives.** Grandparents, aunties and uncles, godparents, teachers, coaches, mentors, neighbors, the friend every kid in the building trusts. If a young person looks to you, you have a parenting role.

**3. Reparenting yourself.** Every one of us was once a child who needed things: safety, encouragement, someone who saw them. Some of us got those things. Some of us didn't. This week, you can begin giving the child you once were what they needed.

## 3. A word of care

For some of us, this week touches grief: a child we've lost, children we hoped for and didn't have, an estrangement that hurts every day, or our own childhood pain.

If that's you, please be gentle with yourself. Take what serves you this week and leave the rest. And if something heavy comes up, reach out to someone you trust, or to a counselor. You don't have to carry it alone.

## 4. Strong enough to hold, open enough to let fly

Here's the image for this week.

A caterpillar can't be *made* into a butterfly. No one can reach into the cocoon and do the work for it. In fact, if you cut the cocoon open to "help", the butterfly's wings never get strong enough to fly.

Parenting is a lot like that. Our job isn't to fly for our children. It's to be **strong enough to hold them** while they grow, and **open enough to let them fly** in their own way, on their own wings. Even when every part of us wants to reach in and help.

## 5. Movement 1: The Air I'm In

Let's look honestly at this part of your life, with a lot of grace. There are no perfect parents, only present ones.

We'll check in on six parts of parenting. If you're reparenting yourself, rate how you treat the child within you.

- **Connection**: do they feel close to me?
- **Presence**: undistracted time together
- **Guidance and boundaries**: clear, loving limits
- **Encouragement**: do they know I believe in them?
- **Modeling**: do they see me live what I teach?
- **Letting them be who they are**: not who I planned or wished

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: The Air I'm In**
> 1. On a scale of 1 to 10, this part of my life today is a ___.
> 2. Rate each of the six parts from 1 to 10.
> 3. Three words the young people in my life would use to describe me…
> 4. The young people in my circle of care are… (If you're reparenting yourself, include you at age ___.)
> 5. What weighs on me here…

## 6. Movement 2: My Truth

We tend to parent the way we were parented, or in exact opposition to it. Either way, our beliefs about raising children were mostly handed to us long before we had any.

See which of these feel familiar:

- "I have to be a perfect parent."
- "My child's choices are a report card on me."
- "It's too late to repair things with my children."
- "I don't have children, so this isn't for me."
- "Repair matters more than perfection."
- "The child I was still deserves care."

Same three questions: **Is it true for me? Is it mine, or was it handed to me? Does it lift me, or weigh me down?**

Then write **My Truth**: what you choose to believe about parenting and about the young lives you influence.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Truth**
> 1. List the beliefs you carry about parenting, and about how you were parented.
> 2. Mark each one: **mine** or **handed to me**, **lifts** or **weighs**.
> 3. Write **My Truth**: three to five sentences, present tense.

## 7. Movement 3: My Horizon

Now picture this part of your life thriving.

If you're raising children: what is your home like? How do you talk to each other? How do you handle hard moments? What do your children feel when they walk through the door? What's your relationship like when they're grown?

If you influence young lives: what kind of presence are you for them? What do they come to you for?

If you're reparenting yourself: how do you speak to yourself when you make a mistake? How do you comfort yourself? What do you finally allow yourself to have?

Then one more question, for everyone: **What do I most want them to carry from me?** Not money or things. The values, the memories, the way of being in the world.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Horizon**
> 1. Describe this part of my life thriving, in the present tense.
> 2. What I most want them to carry from me:

## 8. Movement 4: My Why

Why does this matter to you?

The way you show up for young lives ripples forward for generations, into families and communities you may never see. And the way you care for the child you once were changes how you care for everyone else.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Why**
> 1. This part of my life matters to me because…
> 2. The people who will feel the difference are…
> 3. If nothing changes here, what it costs me, and them, is…

## 9. Movement 5: My Flight Plan

Your Flight Plan this week has four parts. Adapt each one to your way through the week.

- **Presence**: rituals of undistracted time. Bedtime conversations. A weekly one-on-one. A Sunday call with your grown child. Phone-free dinners. For reparenting: time you give to yourself, on purpose.
- **Guidance and boundaries**: the one or two limits that matter most, held with love and consistency.
- **Encouragement and repair**: how you'll say what you believe about them, out loud, and how you'll repair when you get it wrong. "I'm sorry I yelled. You didn't deserve that."
- **My village**: who helps you: a co-parent, family, friends, a parenting group, a counselor, a mentor. No one raises a child alone, and no one should try.

## 10. Movement 6: My Next Right Movement

One thing, this week.

Plan the one-on-one. Make the call. Offer the apology. Put the phone in the drawer at bedtime. Write a letter to your younger self.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Next Right Movement**
> This week, I will… (what, when, and how I'll know I did it)

## 11. Movement 7: Soul Challenge: "Catch Them Shining"

Your Soul Challenge this week is called **Catch Them Shining**.

Each day for seven days, notice one young person in your life doing something good. Not just an achievement. Character: kindness, effort, honesty, courage, patience. Then tell them, specifically. "I saw you share with your brother even though you didn't want to. That was kind."

If you're reparenting yourself, catch *yourself* shining, and tell the child you once were what you see.

Write one line each day: who, what you saw, and what you said.

## 12. Closing

Here's what you did this week. You chose your way through the week. You looked honestly, with grace, at how you help young lives grow. You chose what you believe. You pictured this part of your life thriving and named what you want them to carry. And you built a plan to be present.

That's the seventh chapter of your Charter.

Bring your Catch Them Shining notes to this week's Gathering. What did you see when you looked for it?

Next week, we close the Circle with your **Social Life**: your friendships, your community, and the people you choose to travel with.

Head up. Wings out.
