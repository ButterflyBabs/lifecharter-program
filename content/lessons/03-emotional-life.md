## 1. Welcome to the heart of the Cocoon

Welcome back. This is our last week in the Cocoon, and in many ways it's the heart of it.

We've looked at your connection to something larger than you. We've looked at the person you're choosing to be. This week we turn to what moves through you every single day: your **Emotional Life**.

This is how you feel, what you do with what you feel, and how you begin to heal what's been holding you back.

I'll be honest with you. This can be the most tender week of the whole program. It's also the one where I've seen the most people start to fly.

## 2. A word of care before we begin

Before we go any further, I want to say something important.

This program is a place for reflection and growth. It isn't therapy, and it isn't a substitute for care from a counselor or doctor. If something heavy comes up for you this week, please be gentle with yourself and reach out: to someone you trust, to your counselor, or to a professional.

If you're ever in crisis, please contact your local emergency services or a crisis line right away. In the US, you can call or text 988.

You never have to carry the heavy things alone. That's true in this program, and it's true in your life.

## 3. Feelings are the air

Here's the image I want you to hold this week.

Your emotions are the air you fly through. Some days there's a gentle lift under your wings. Some days there's a headwind. Some days a storm rolls through that you never saw coming.

You can't stop the wind. No one can. What you *can* do is learn to read it, and to fly with it instead of fighting it every moment. That's renegotiating with the air.

Feelings are **information, not instructions**. Fear tells you something matters. Anger tells you a line was crossed. Grief tells you how much you loved. Sadness asks you to slow down. Joy tells you you're home. None of them are bad. And none of them get to take the controls.

## 4. Movement 1: The Air I'm In

Let's look honestly at your emotional life right now. Not how it looks to other people. Not how you'd like it to be. How it actually is.

What feelings visit you most often these days? What do you do when a hard feeling shows up? Some of us stuff it down. Some of us explode. Some of us numb out with food, a screen, work, or keeping busy. Some of us talk it through, pray, write or cry. Most of us do a mix.

No judgment here. Every one of those habits made sense at some point. Many of them helped you survive. We're just looking.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: The Air I'm In**
> 1. On a scale of 1 to 10, my emotional life today is a ___.
> 2. The three feelings that visit me most often these days are…
> 3. When a hard feeling shows up, what I usually do is…
> 4. Is there an old hurt I'm still carrying? You can name it here, or just note that it's there.

## 5. Movement 2: My Truth

Many of the beliefs we carry about feelings were handed to us very early. We learned them at the dinner table, on the playground, from watching how the grown-ups handled things, or didn't.

See which of these feel familiar:

- "Strong people don't cry."
- "If I let myself feel it, I'll fall apart."
- "Other people are responsible for how I feel."
- "Anger is always bad."
- "Feelings are information, not instructions."
- "Healing isn't linear, and it's allowed to take time."

Same three questions for each belief: **Is it true for me? Is it mine, or was it handed to me? Does it lift me, or weigh me down?**

Then write **My Truth**: what you choose to believe about your feelings, and about your capacity to heal.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Truth**
> 1. List the beliefs you carry about feelings, and about yourself when you feel them.
> 2. Mark each one: **mine** or **handed to me**, **lifts** or **weighs**.
> 3. Write **My Truth**: three to five sentences, present tense.

## 6. Movement 3: My Horizon, and the weight I'm ready to set down

Now let's picture your emotional life thriving.

Not a life without hard feelings. That's not a real life. A life where you feel what you feel, understand what it's telling you, and respond instead of react. Where joy has room. Where you can be with someone else's feelings without drowning in them.

How do you meet a hard day? How do you express anger, or hurt, in a way you're proud of? How much room is there for joy, delight and laughter? What have you made peace with?

Then one more piece, and this one is gentle and completely optional.

Is there **a weight you're ready to set down**? An old hurt, a resentment, a story about yourself, a grief you've been holding with clenched hands? You don't have to fix it this week. You don't even have to be ready to forgive anyone. You're just naming what you're ready to begin setting down. Wings fly better when they're not carrying a load.

If you're not ready, that's okay. Write "not yet". That's honest, and honesty is enough.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Horizon**
> 1. Describe my emotional life thriving, in the present tense: hard days, anger and hurt, joy, peace.
> 2. The weight I'm ready to begin setting down is… (or "not yet")

## 7. Movement 4: My Why

Why does this matter to you?

Your emotional life is the weather inside every other dimension. It shapes how you love, how you parent, how you work, how you rest, and whether you can actually enjoy the life you're building.

Think about who feels your weather: your partner, your children, your friends, your coworkers. And think about how different your days could feel.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Why**
> 1. A thriving emotional life matters to me because…
> 2. The people who will feel the difference are…
> 3. If nothing changes here, what it costs me is…

## 8. Movement 5: My Flight Plan

Your Flight Plan this week is about building a few steady practices for the weather, so you're not just hoping for calm skies.

Four kinds of practices to consider:

- **A daily weather check**: once or twice a day, pause and name what you're feeling in one word. Naming a feeling gives you a little space from it.
- **A plan for storms**: what you'll do when a big feeling hits. For example: pause, breathe slowly three times, name it, then ask, *"What do I need right now?"*
- **Healthy outlets**: the ways that actually help you move feelings through. Writing, music, prayer, time outside, talking, creating, crying. Choose what works for your life and your body.
- **Support**: who you can call. A friend, a counselor, a group. Healing happens in connection.

Keep it wing-sized.

> 1. My daily weather check:
> 2. My plan for storms:
> 3. My healthy outlets:
> 4. My support, the people I'll reach for:

## 9. Movement 6: My Next Right Movement

One thing, this week. Small, specific and doable.

It might be starting your weather check. It might be telling one person how you're really doing. It might be booking the appointment you've been putting off. It might be letting yourself cry.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Next Right Movement**
> This week, I will… (what, when, and how I'll know I did it)

## 10. Movement 7: Soul Challenge: "Name the Weather"

Your Soul Challenge this week is called **Name the Weather**.

Three times a day, morning, midday and evening, pause for one breath and ask:

- *What am I feeling?* Use one word. There's a list of feeling words in your handout if you need help.
- *Where do I feel it in my body?*
- *What is it asking for?*

Write one line for each day. By the end of the week you'll start to see your own patterns, and patterns are where healing begins.

## 11. Closing

Here's what you did this week. You looked honestly at your emotional weather. You chose what you believe about your feelings and your healing. You pictured your emotional life thriving, and maybe you named a weight you're ready to set down. And you built a plan for the storms.

With this, you've finished the Cocoon. Spiritual Life, Character, Emotional Life: the inside work, done first, on purpose. Three chapters of your Charter.

Bring your Name the Weather notes to this week's Gathering. What pattern did you notice?

Next week, we come out of the Cocoon and into **The Vessel**, starting with your **Health & Fitness**: your body, exactly as it is today, and how to care for it well.

Please be gentle with yourself this week.

Head up. Wings out.
