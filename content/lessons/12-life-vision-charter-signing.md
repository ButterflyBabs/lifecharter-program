## 1. Welcome to Wings Out

Welcome to **Wings Out**.

Twelve weeks ago, we started with "Head up": remembering who you are before your circumstances try to tell you. Since then, you've traveled through the Cocoon, the Vessel, the Circle and the Flight. You've written eleven chapters of your Charter, one honest week at a time.

This week, we bring it all together. You'll write your **Life Vision**, the dimension that holds all the others. And then you'll do something most people never do in their whole lives: you'll **sign your LifeCharter**, the founding document of the life you're choosing on purpose.

Before we do any of that, I want you to look back.

## 2. Look how far you've traveled

Find your Starting Snapshot from Week 0, where you rated all twelve dimensions from 1 to 10. Don't look at it yet.

First, rate all twelve again, today, honestly. Then put the two side by side.

Some numbers will have moved a lot. Some may have barely moved, or even gone down, because you finally saw them clearly. All of that is growth. Clear sight is always the first movement.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Ending Snapshot**
> 1. Rate each of the 12 dimensions from 1 to 10, as it is today.
> 2. Copy your Week 0 scores next to them.
> 3. Where I've grown the most…
> 4. What surprised me…

## 3. Life Vision: the thread through every chapter

Life Vision is the twelfth dimension, and it's different from the others. It isn't one area of your life. It's **the whole of your life, seen at once**: the thread that runs through every chapter, and the direction all your wings are pointing.

Think back to the butterfly with the damaged wing. It doesn't fly by fixing one wing at a time. It flies when the whole body, both wings, and the air all work together. That's what a Life Vision does. It brings your twelve dimensions into one flight.

[BABS: your own Life Vision, in a few sentences. Where you're flying, and how renegotiating with the air shaped it. Only what you want to share.]

We'll move through the same movements one last time, in a slightly different form.

## 4. Movement 1: The Air I'm In, reading your Charter

Gather all eleven of your chapter pages. Read them, slowly, from Spiritual Life to Quality of Life.

As you read, notice what keeps showing up. Which words repeat? Which values appear in chapter after chapter? What dream keeps surfacing, maybe in different clothes?

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: The Themes in My Charter**
> 1. Words and values that show up again and again…
> 2. A dream that keeps surfacing…
> 3. Where my chapters pull against each other, and need to make peace…

## 5. Movement 2: My Life Truth

Across eleven weeks, you sorted beliefs: mine or handed to me, lifting or weighing. Now look for the few truths underneath all the others. These are the beliefs that, if you held onto them, would carry you through anything.

Write your **Life Truth**: three to five sentences about who you are and how you choose to live.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Life Truth**
> Three to five sentences, present tense: the truths that run through every chapter.

## 6. Movement 3: My Horizon, a letter from my future self

Here's something a little different for your Horizon.

Imagine it's the day of your **third Charter Renewal**, three years from now. You're sitting down with your Charter, as you'll do every year. And your future self writes a letter back to you, today.

What would they tell you? What has happened in their life, across all twelve dimensions? What were they so glad you started? What did they stop worrying about? What do they want you to know about the air ahead?

Write it as a letter: "Dear me…" Let it be specific, warm and honest.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: A Letter from My Future Self**
> Written on the day of my third Charter Renewal. Begin with "Dear me…"

## 7. Movement 4: My Why, my Charter Statement

Now let's distill it all into a sentence or two you can carry with you every day.

Your **Charter Statement** answers the biggest "why" of all: **what is my life for?** It might begin "I am here to…" or "My life is about…" It doesn't need to be grand. It needs to be true.

Take your time. Write a few versions. Keep the one that makes something in you go quiet and say *yes*.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Charter Statement**
> One or two sentences: what my life is for.

## 8. Movement 5: My Flight Plan, keeping the Charter alive

A charter that lives in a drawer can't guide a life. So your final Flight Plan has two parts.

**First, your next ninety days.** You can't pursue twelve dimensions' worth of goals at once, and you don't have to. Choose **three priorities** from across your Charter for the next ninety days. The ones that would give you the most lift.

**Second, the rhythm that keeps your Charter alive:**

- **Weekly**: a few minutes to read one chapter and check your next right movement.
- **Monthly**: a longer look. What's working? What needs adjusting?
- **Yearly**: your **Charter Renewal**. On the anniversary of today, you'll sit down with your Charter, rate your twelve dimensions again, and update each chapter for the year ahead. Put the date in your calendar today.

> 1. My three priorities for the next 90 days:
> 2. My weekly, monthly and yearly rhythm:
> 3. My Charter Renewal date:

## 9. Movement 6: My Next Right Movement

One thing, this week. Maybe the first step on priority number one. Maybe putting your Renewal date on the calendar. Maybe reading your Charter Statement out loud to someone you love.

> **⏸ PAUSE & WRITE: My Next Right Movement**
> This week, I will… (what, when, and how I'll know I did it)

## 10. Movement 7: Soul Challenge: "Wings Out"

Your final Soul Challenge is called **Wings Out**.

Each morning for seven days, read your Charter Statement out loud before your day begins. Then choose one moment that day to live it on purpose, and write one line about it.

And at least once this week, share your Charter Statement with someone: a person you love, a fellow traveler, or the Collective. Something changes when we say out loud who we're choosing to be.

## 11. Signing your LifeCharter

And now, the moment we've been traveling toward.

At this week's Gathering, we'll hold our **Charter Signing** together. Here's what to bring: all twelve of your chapter pages, your Charter Statement, and the signing page from your handout.

Here's how the signing works:

1. **Read your Charter Statement out loud.** Even if your voice shakes. Especially if it does.
2. **Read the charter declaration** on your signing page.
3. **Sign and date it.**
4. If you'd like, ask a **witness** to sign too: a fellow traveler, your partner, a friend. Someone who will remind you of it when the air gets rough.

If you can't be with us live, hold your own signing. Light a candle, play a song that means something to you, and do it with intention. Then tell us in the Collective. We'll celebrate you.

## 12. Graduation, and what's next

When you sign your Charter, you graduate from the LifeCharter Program. But your Charter isn't finished. It's a living document, and you're just getting started.

Here's what comes next:

- **Your Charter Renewal**, every year on your signing anniversary.
- **The LifeCharter Collective**, where you stay connected to your fellow travelers as an alumnus.
- **A complimentary Next Chapter Call with me.** Thirty minutes to talk about where you're flying next.

And if your Charter showed you that you're building something, like a business or a mission of your own, I'd love to help you build it. LifeCharter graduates who enroll in Command Suite implementation within 30 days of graduating receive a $500 alumni credit. We'll talk about it on your Next Chapter Call.

## 13. Closing

[BABS: your graduation message to this group, in your own words.]

Here's what you did. For twelve weeks you told the truth about your life, one dimension at a time. You chose what you believe. You pictured your life thriving. You found your why. You built flight plans sized to your own wings. And you took your next right movement, again and again.

You don't need a perfect wing. You don't need a windless day. You don't need the entire flight plan. You have enough clarity now to see your next right movement, and I've watched you find the courage to extend your wings into it.

I'm so honored to have traveled this path beside you.

Head up. Wings out.
